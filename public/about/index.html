About

I am a PhD student in Biostatistics at the University of Iowa, where I am receiving rigorous training in statistics and gaining valuable research experience through collaborations with researchers from other disciplines. I work as a research assistant in the Biostatistics Core at the Holden Comprehensive Cancer Center. I have been fortunate to receive guidance from Drs. Emily Roberts, Patrick Breheny, Gideon Zamba and David Zahrieh.

My undergraduate major was Financial Engineering (within the field of Economics), and I previously worked as a quantitative researcher, focusing on high-frequency trading using methods such as stochastic analysis and reinforcement learning. Over time, I realized that I was more interested in applying statistical methods to address biomedical problems.

My current research interests include Clinical Trials (dose selection/optimization, seamless designs, and patient-focused drug development), Bayesian Methods (Bayesian adaptive designs and Bayesian borrowing), and Causal Inference (indirect comparisons, covariate adjustment, and surrogate endpoint validation). Some of these topics are related to my previous/current work or will be explored in my PhD dissertation research.
